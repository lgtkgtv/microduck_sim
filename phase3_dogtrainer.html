1. The Gym Rules: Observation & Action 🏋️

In Reinforcement Learning, we don't code walking trajectories. We define what the robot sees (Observation) and what it can move (Action).

The 12YO Concept: If you train a dog, you don't pick up its paws. You show it the treat, observe what it does, and give feedback.

👁️ Observation (s_t)
• IMU Orient: [φ, θ, ψ]
• Gyroscope: [ωx, ωy, ωz]
• Joint Encoders: 15 angles
• 4-Frame Buffer: 60 Floats
➔
⚡ Action (a_t)
• 15 Motor Targets
• Normalized: [-1.0, 1.0]
• Rate: 50 Hz (20ms)
• Mode: PD Velocity

Cadence: Observation in → ONNX Inference (2.5ms) → Action out → Sleep (17.5ms)

2. The Reward Function: Biscuit Math 🍪

The AI is greedy—it will exploit any loophole to maximize score. We must balance positive progress against destructive motor thrashing.

The 12YO Concept: If you only give points for moving fast, the duck might throw itself down the stairs. You must penalize falling and motor shaking.

3. Actor-Critic & PPO: Coach vs Athlete 🥋

PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) uses two neural networks working together: the Athlete (Actor) and the Coach (Critic).

The 12YO Concept: The Athlete tries a cartwheel. The Coach rates how good it was. PPO stops the Athlete from making crazy changes that erase what it learned.

PPO Ratio r(θ): 1.00 (Clipped Zone: 1-ε to 1+ε)

✅ Within Safe Clipping Envelope: Smooth gradient updates.

4. The Hardware Clamp: Silicon Safety 🛡️

Neural networks can accidentally output wild numbers like +5.8 or -10.2. On a physical robot, this would strip the nylon gears instantly.

The 12YO Concept: We bake torch.clamp() directly into the ONNX brain so that no matter what, the math physically cannot exceed [-1.0, 1.0].

Raw AI Output:
+2.40
➔ torch.clamp(-1, 1) ➔
Motor Signal:
+1.00

Mathematical guarantee: 100% immune to gear stripping in hardware.
