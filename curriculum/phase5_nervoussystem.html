1. Rust & Embedded Memory Safety 🦀

Real-time robots cannot afford crashes or Garbage Collection pauses. Pollen Robotics chose Rust for the Microduck's core daemon stack.

The 12YO Concept: In a library, you can only borrow one book at a time. The borrow checker ensures no two motors ever fight over the same wire.

❌ C/C++ Danger:
• Null pointer segfault
• Data race on /dev/imu
• Robot falls mid-step
✅ Rust Compiler:
• Strict Ownership model
• Thread-safe Send/Sync
• Zero runtime GC pauses

Compile error prevented: cannot borrow `imu_data` as mutable more than once at a time

2. The robotd Daemon: The Spinal Cord ⚡

robotd is the central system daemon running on the Rockchip RK3566. It polls sensors, executes ONNX inference, and fires 15 motors every 20ms.

The 12YO Concept: Your spinal cord makes your leg kick when tapped before your brain even thinks. robotd handles these fast reflexes.

Daemon: robotd.service
PID: 1042 (Real-Time SCHED_FIFO)
● ACTIVE (50.0 Hz)
Jitter: ±0.08 ms
Drop Rate: 0.00%

Step sequence: read_sensors() → onnx_step() → write_actuators() → timer.sleep_until(20ms)

3. Inter-Process Communication: Unix Sockets 🔌

How do we talk to robotd without interrupting the 50Hz balance loop? Through an asynchronous Unix Domain Socket (/var/run/robotd.sock).

The 12YO Concept: Like slipping a written note under a locked classroom door without making the teacher pause the lecture.

CLI / App Socket
JSON Request:
{"cmd": "get_telemetry"}
⇄
robotd Daemon
Telemetry Stream:
{"imu_pitch": -1.2, "batt": 88%}

Zero-copy local memory transfers keep communication latency under 0.1ms.

5. Configuration: The configd Daemon 📐

No two 3D-printed parts are identical! configd manages motor zero-offsets, PID gains, and sensor calibration matrices.

The 12YO Concept: If your bicycle handlebars are slightly crooked from the factory, you adjust the screw so it drives straight. That is what config offsets do.

Raw Zero: -0.08 rad
Offset ($\delta$): +0.08 rad
Calibrated Zero: 0.00 rad (True Neutral)

Persistent config saved: /etc/microduck/calibration.json
